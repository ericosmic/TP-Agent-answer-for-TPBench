# Step 2 — Use energy conservation for a time-independent Hamiltonian: since $H$ has no explicit time dependence, $\langle H\rangle_t=\langle\psi(t)|H|\psi(t)\rangle=\langle\psi(0)|H|\psi(0)\rangle$.

## Solution Reasoning

For any time-independent Hamiltonian, the expectation value of the Hamiltonian is a constant of motion. This follows either from Ehrenfest's theorem or directly from unitary evolution $|\psi(t)\rangle=e^{-iHt/\hbar}|\psi(0)\rangle$, because $H$ commutes with its own time-evolution operator. Thus the potentially nontrivial oscillations between basis states $|1\rangle$ and $|3\rangle$ do not change the total energy expectation. The prior block-structure observation is compatible with this, but not necessary for proving conservation of $\langle H\rangle$.

## Result

Since the Hamiltonian has no explicit time dependence, the expectation value of energy is conserved. The Schrodinger evolution is generated by the same operator $H$, so $$\frac{d}{dt}\langle H\rangle_t = \frac{i}{\hbar}\langle [H,H]\rangle_t + \left\langle \frac{\partial H}{\partial t}\right\rangle_t = 0.$$ Therefore $$\langle H\rangle_t=\langle\psi(t)|H|\psi(t)\rangle=\langle\psi(0)|H|\psi(0)\rangle.$$ For the present initial state this means the energy expectation can be evaluated entirely at $t=0$; no explicit solution for $|\psi(t)\rangle$ is needed in this step.
