# answer2_error_analysis
 Answer2 gives $\delta_\eta\phi=+\sqrt{2}\eta\xi$. With the transformation rules stated in the problem and the standard identity $\bar\sigma^\mu\sigma^\nu+\bar\sigma^\nu\sigma^\mu=2g^{\mu\nu}$, the derivative terms from the fermion kinetic variation are cancelled only by $\delta_\eta\phi=-\sqrt{2}\eta\xi$. Answer2 effectively uses the opposite sign in the symmetric part of the sigma-matrix product, e.g. $\sigma^\nu\bar\sigma^\mu=-g^{\nu\mu}+\cdots$, which is inconsistent with the conventions implicit in the given calculation. Its barred expression $+\sqrt{2}\bar\eta\bar\xi$ can be equivalent to $-\sqrt{2}\bar\xi\bar\eta$ after Grassmann reordering, but the unbarred transformation has the wrong sign.

# answer2_math_proof
 Let the unknown scalar variation be $\delta_\eta\phi=a\,\eta\xi$ and $\delta_\eta\bar\phi=b\,\bar\xi\bar\eta$, keeping the ordering used in Answer1. The part of the fermion kinetic variation depending on $\partial\phi$ is
$$
\delta\mathcal L_f\big|_{\partial\phi}=\sqrt{2}\eta\sigma^\nu\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\nu\bar\phi\,\partial_\mu\xi-\sqrt{2}\bar\xi\bar\sigma^\mu\sigma^\nu\bar\eta\,\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi.
$$
Integrating the second term by parts and using the symmetric part of the sigma identities,
$$
\sigma^\nu\bar\sigma^\mu+\sigma^\mu\bar\sigma^\nu=2g^{\mu\nu},\qquad
\bar\sigma^\mu\sigma^\nu+\bar\sigma^\nu\sigma^\mu=2g^{\mu\nu},
$$
the antisymmetric pieces are total derivatives, and therefore, modulo total derivatives,
$$
\delta\mathcal L_f\big|_{\partial\phi}\simeq \sqrt{2}\eta\partial_\mu\bar\phi\,\partial^\mu\xi+\sqrt{2}\partial^\mu\bar\xi\,\bar\eta\,\partial_\mu\phi.
$$
The scalar kinetic variation gives
$$
\delta\mathcal L_s=\partial_\mu\bar\phi\,\partial^\mu(a\eta\xi)+\partial_\mu(b\bar\xi\bar\eta)\partial^\mu\phi
=a\eta\partial_\mu\xi\,\partial^\mu\bar\phi+b\partial_\mu\bar\xi\,\bar\eta\,\partial^\mu\phi.
$$
Since $\partial_\mu\bar\phi$ and $\partial_\mu\phi$ are bosonic, cancellation requires
$$
\sqrt{2}+a=0,\qquad \sqrt{2}+b=0,
$$
so
$$
a=b=-\sqrt{2}.
$$
Thus
$$
\delta_\eta\phi=-\sqrt{2}\eta\xi,\qquad \delta_\eta\bar\phi=-\sqrt{2}\bar\xi\bar\eta.
$$
If Answer2's $a=+\sqrt{2}$ is used instead, the unbarred sector leaves the nonzero remainder
$$
2\sqrt{2}\eta\partial_\mu\xi\,\partial^\mu\bar\phi,
$$
whose Euler derivative with respect to $\bar\phi$ is $-2\sqrt{2}\eta\Box\xi$, not identically zero. Therefore it is not merely a total derivative and the action is not invariant.
