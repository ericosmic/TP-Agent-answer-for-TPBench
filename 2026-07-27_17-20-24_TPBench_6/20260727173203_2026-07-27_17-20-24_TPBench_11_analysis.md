# answer2_error_analysis
 Answer2 correctly integrates the scale factor and obtains the same leading exponential constant as Answer1, since $\Gamma(5/4)=\Gamma(1/4)/4$. However, it chooses the upper-half-plane turning point and then imposes exponential suppression by hand. For the phase factor $\exp[-2i\int^\eta \omega d\eta]$, the large-$|\eta|$ convergence deformation relevant to the one-pole contribution is into the lower half-plane, selecting the conjugate turning point with $\Re\tilde\eta>0$ and $\Im\tilde\eta<0$. More importantly, Answer2 treats $\omega'_k/(2\omega_k)$ near a zero of $\omega_k^2$ as if it were an ordinary meromorphic pole enclosed by a full closed contour, giving a residue factor $2\pi i\cdot(1/4)=i\pi/2$. This is not the steepest-descent one-pole factor for this problem. The point is a square-root branch point of $\omega_k$, and the local exponential contains $(\eta-\tilde\eta)^{3/2}$. The steepest contour does not encircle the singularity through a full $2\pi$ angle; only the allowed branch-cut/steepest-descent angular sector contributes. This gives $|v|=\pi/3$, not $\pi/2$. Therefore Answer2's final prefactor is wrong, although its exponent is equivalent to Answer1's exponent.

# answer2_math_proof
 Let $Q(\eta)=\omega_k^2(\eta)$. At a simple zero $\tilde\eta$ of $Q$, write $\xi=\eta-\tilde\eta$. Then

$$
Q(\eta)=Q'(\tilde\eta)\xi+O(\xi^2),\qquad
\omega_k(\eta)\sim [Q'(\tilde\eta)]^{1/2}\xi^{1/2}.
$$

Thus

$$
\frac{\omega_k'}{2\omega_k}=\frac14\frac{Q'}{Q}
\sim \frac{1}{4\xi}.
$$

But the local phase is not analytic-meromorphic around $\xi=0$:

$$
\int_{\tilde\eta}^{\eta}\omega_k(\eta')d\eta'
\sim \frac23[Q'(\tilde\eta)]^{1/2}\xi^{3/2}.
$$

Hence the local one-pole integral has the form

$$
v=\int_{\mathcal C}\frac{d\xi}{4\xi}
\exp\left[-\frac43 i [Q'(\tilde\eta)]^{1/2}\xi^{3/2}\right],
$$

up to the irrelevant common phase. Because of the fractional power $\xi^{3/2}$, $\xi=0$ is a branch point, not an ordinary isolated meromorphic pole. Therefore Cauchy's residue theorem with a full $2\pi$ encirclement is inapplicable.

For the lower-half-plane dominant saddle used by the convergent deformation of $\exp[-2i\int\omega d\eta]$, the steepest-descent contour approaches and leaves the branch point along the rays

$$
\theta_1=\frac{5\pi}{4},\qquad \theta_2=-\frac{\pi}{12},
$$

with $\xi=\epsilon e^{i\theta}$ on the small connecting arc. The radial pieces cancel in the $\epsilon\to0$ limit, while the small arc gives

$$
v=\frac14\int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2} i\,d\theta
=\frac{i}{4}\left(-\frac{\pi}{12}-\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)
=-\frac{i\pi}{3}.
$$

Thus

$$
|v|=\frac{\pi}{3},
$$

not $\pi/2$. This proves that Answer2's prefactor is mathematically incorrect.

The exponential part in Answer2 can be compared with Answer1 as follows. Answer2's exponent coefficient is

$$
\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\Gamma(1/4)}{3\Gamma(3/4)}.
$$

Answer1's coefficient is

$$
\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma(5/4)}{\Gamma(3/4)}
=\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma(1/4)/4}{\Gamma(3/4)}
=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\Gamma(1/4)}{3\Gamma(3/4)}.
$$

The powers also agree:

$$
\frac{k}{a_eH_I}\left(\frac{k}{ma_e}\right)^{1/2}
=\frac{(k/a_e)^{3/2}}{H_I\sqrt m}.
$$

Therefore the only leading asymptotic discrepancy is the prefactor, and the correct one-pole result is

$$
|\beta(k)|\approx \frac{\pi}{3}\exp\left[-\frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma(5/4)}{\Gamma(3/4)}\frac{(k/a_e)^{3/2}}{H_I\sqrt m}\right].
$$
