# answer2_error_analysis
 Answer2 correctly obtains the vertex $-i\lambda$, the tree amplitude $\mathcal{M}=-\lambda$, and the CM momentum ratio $|\vec{k}_1'|/|\vec{k}_1|=\sqrt{(s-4m_2^2)/(s-4m_1^2)}$. Its error is inserting an additional factor $1/2!$ into $d\sigma/d\Omega$. For the usual differential cross section with respect to the solid angle of one chosen outgoing momentum, the standard two-body formula is $$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{1}{64\pi^2s}\frac{|\vec{k}_f|}{|\vec{k}_i|}|\mathcal{M}|^2.$$ The identical-particle factor is relevant when computing the fully integrated cross section over the full two-particle phase space as an unordered final state, or equivalently when avoiding double counting in total phase-space integration. It is not an extra factor in the conventional $d\sigma/d\Omega$ formula used for the angular distribution of one outgoing particle. Therefore Answer2 is smaller than the standard result by a factor of $2$.

# answer2_math_proof
 The interaction term is $$\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=-\frac{\lambda}{4}\phi_1^2\phi_2^2=-\frac{\lambda}{2!2!}\phi_1^2\phi_2^2,$$ so the four-point Feynman rule for two $\phi_1$ and two $\phi_2$ legs is $$-i\lambda.$$ Thus, at tree level, $$i\mathcal{M}=-i\lambda,\qquad |\mathcal{M}|^2=\lambda^2.$$ The Lorentz-invariant two-body phase-space calculation gives, in the CM frame, $$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{1}{64\pi^2s}\frac{|\vec{k}_f|}{|\vec{k}_i|}|\mathcal{M}|^2.$$ Now $$\sqrt{s}=2\sqrt{|\vec{k}_i|^2+m_1^2}=2\sqrt{|\vec{k}_f|^2+m_2^2},$$ hence $$|\vec{k}_i|=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{s-4m_1^2},\qquad |\vec{k}_f|=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{s-4m_2^2}.$$ Therefore $$\frac{|\vec{k}_f|}{|\vec{k}_i|}=\sqrt{\frac{s-4m_2^2}{s-4m_1^2}},$$ and the standard CM differential cross section is $$\boxed{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2s}\sqrt{\frac{s-4m_2^2}{s-4m_1^2}}}.$$ Answer2 instead gives $$\frac{\lambda^2}{128\pi^2s}\sqrt{\frac{s-4m_2^2}{s-4m_1^2}},$$ which differs by the factor $$\frac{1}{2}.$$ Hence Answer2 is mathematically inconsistent with the standard differential two-body formula for $d\sigma/d\Omega$ as requested.
