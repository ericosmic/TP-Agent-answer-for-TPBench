# answer2_math_proof
 Let \(f(\eta)=\omega_k'(\eta)/(2\omega_k(\eta))\). The problem asks for the contribution obtained by deforming the contour to the dominant pole \(\tilde\eta\) of \(f\), i.e. the local singularity of the analytic continuation of the integrand. Near a simple zero of \(\omega_k^2\), one has
\[
\omega_k^2(\eta)=\omega_k^2{}'(\tilde\eta)(\eta-\tilde\eta)+O((\eta-\tilde\eta)^2),
\]
so
\[
\frac{\omega_k'}{2\omega_k}=
\frac{1}{4}\frac{(\omega_k^2)'}{\omega_k^2}
=\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{\eta-\tilde\eta}+O(1).
\]
Thus the one-pole method gives a single residue contribution multiplied by the steepest-descent exponential. Any extra term coming from the nonanalyticity at the transition surface is not the same object; it is a separate boundary contribution from a discontinuity in derivatives of \(a(\eta)\). Therefore the statement in Answer2 that the exact sharp model is dominated in the strict UV by a \(k^{-4}\) boundary tail is irrelevant to the asked one-pole approximation.

More concretely, the contour deformation prescription in the problem already fixes the approximation class: the answer must be of the form
\[
|\beta(k)|\sim C\,\exp\!\Big(-2\,\mathrm{Im}\!\int^{\tilde\eta}\!\omega_k\,d\eta\Big)
\]
with a single constant prefactor \(C\) determined by the pole contour. Answer2 instead returns
\[
|\beta(k)|\simeq |\beta_{\rm bdry}(k)|+|\beta_{\rm pole}(k)|,
\]
which is a different asymptotic expansion and therefore does not satisfy the problem statement. Hence Answer2 is not the correct response.
